## Narrative: A home that holds its place in the city

On Ambli-Bopal Road, Parijaat Eclat is not trying to be a skyline gesture first. It is a place to live. Forty-eight homes, stacked through twenty-three floors, give the project a scarcity that residents can feel: fewer doors on the corridor, more of the building belonging to the people who live there.

The apartments and penthouses are planned as luxury 4 BHK homes. That is the client value, stated plainly. A four-bedroom plan is a family plan — rooms that can hold parents, children, guests, and the quieter work of daily life — rather than a show flat stretched across a typical floor. The penthouses sit at the top of that same promise: more of the same home, not a different building pretending to be one.

The facade is exposed concrete. In Ahmedabad that material is not a fashion. It is a way of making a high-rise feel solid in the hand and honest in the light. For the people who live here, the building does not hide behind a borrowed skin. It keeps a calm face to the road and a durable one to the weather.

Parijaat Eclat is also designed as part of a larger piece of the city. The project sheet records its integration with the Privilon commercial hub. That matters at the scale of a day. Home and work sit in the same stretch of Ambli-Bopal Road, so the journey between them can stay short, and the residential address does not have to turn its back on the life of the corridor. The commercial neighbour is a fact of the campus, not a claim about this tower’s interiors.

Where collaboration enters the story, it is only as the yaml names it. Benoy is credited as exterior designer. HCP Interior Design is credited for sample apartments. Those credits are listed as written. They are not expanded here into campus-wide authorship, interior design of every home, or a joint-venture narrative. Principle Architect review should confirm whether Benoy’s exterior role on Parijaat Eclat is independent of Benoy’s separately recorded exterior role on Privilon, and whether HCP’s credit stops, as written, at the sample apartments.

The rest of the named team is equally specific. Landscape is credited to Prakruti Landscapes. MEP is credited to Chettiar Consulting Engineers. Both belong in the record because they are how a luxury high-rise actually works for the people inside it: the ground that greets you, and the services you should never have to think about. No further landscape story, no invented service strategy, is added here.

This is Vitan Architects’ work, led by Ar. Jagrut Patel. The firm’s measure is simple: adding life, every square foot — not by claiming more building than the facts allow, but by making the homes that exist feel complete.
